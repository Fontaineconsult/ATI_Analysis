# SFSU Instructional Materials 2025-2026: Evidence Summaries
Draft for review, 2026-10-28


## Goal 1: Timely Adoption of Instructional Materials

### 1.1-ins — Initiated
A subcommittee of the Bookstore Advisory Committee restored the timely adoption communication process in 2026 after it lapsed during COVID. Follett now sends adoption deadline messages and missing-adoption reports directly to Deans each term. Fall 2026 adoptions rose from 60% to 80%. The Spring 2027 cycle is already running, with messages sent before the October 15 deadline. The group settled on a hybrid model: Follett runs operational communication with faculty and Deans, and Academic Affairs forwards the Chancellor's Office memo each term. Chris Farmer is drafting a summary document of the work done and what remains. The Faculty Affairs Senate Committee is separately reviewing and updating Policy S08-249 (Timely Adoption of Textbooks for Students). No finalized policy or resolution has been adopted yet. The formal process document and semester adoption reports the Chancellor's Office looks for as evidence are not on file.

### 1.2-ins — Initiated
Responsibility for the timely-adoption compliance process belonged to the Associate Vice President for Academic Affairs Operations. That position no longer exists. Re-establishing the process depends on progress under 1.1-ins, and no staff time, training or technology has been assigned.

### 1.5-ins — Not Started
No evidence of any kind exists for this indicator. No person, process or report addresses the distribution of timely-adoption performance data to campus administration.

### 1.6-ins — Initiated
Follett sent the Deans a missing-adoption report on July 30, 2026 listing over 1,600 sections without course material selections for Fall 2026, broken out by department. A Spring 2027 version is in process. Chris Farmer is working with Andrew Roderick to formalize the campus side: the goal is a Dean's Council presentation and Provost's office ownership of forwarding the Chancellor's Office memo each term. The Follett Discover app in SF State Gateway may hold additional reporting data, but the campus has not investigated it. The distribution is happening through Follett, but no campus-owned process governs the cadence or recipients.

## Goal 4: Accessible Course Content in the LMS

### 4.1-ins — Defined
LMS Promotional Materials and the JEDI PIE certificate series address posting instructional materials to Canvas. About 50 faculty have completed Slice 2 of JEDI PIE, which carries UDL 3.0 content sourced from ATI websites across the CSU system. Public guidance about using the LMS exists in several places. No internal process document governs the activity, and no implementation on this indicator has an owner.

### 4.3-ins — Defined
Five implementations address accessible course content in Canvas, among them public accessibility guidance, AT's Canvas remediation process, the Accessible Media Quick Converter and CEETL courses. Canvas Studio now supports professional captioning through Verbit. AT is building an accessibility training portal, but nothing is public yet. AT is not initially collaborating with CEETL on training during the current organizational transition.

### 4.5-ins — Defined
DPRC uses a custom application (Canvas Bot) that scans courses daily when a student requests accommodation. Alt media production staff get immediate access to files flagged for remediation. Two dedicated Accessible Technologists, Ray Choudhury and Dalice Mariah Stogden, hold this work in their position descriptions. The Accessible Media Quick Converter handles format conversion. Canvas Bot currently serves direct accommodations only, not universal design.

### 4.11-ins — Initiated
AT manages accessibility for faculty web applications used in instruction and assesses compliance at procurement. Third-party tools that faculty select outside AT's support still require review, and some publisher tools bundled with textbook packages bypass that review entirely. The campus is examining options but has no documented process.


## Goal 5: Instructional Media Types

### 5.11-ins — Defined
Five active implementations address audio and video accessibility: Canvas Studio professional captioning through Verbit (deployed this year), JEDI PIE, the Accessible Media Quick Converter, CPaGE processes, and the Canvas remediation process. Cristian Alvarado operates the Verbit integration and handles one-off caption requests. Kevin Connor, Alex Cherian, Ash Klein, and Daniel Fontaine also implement. CPaGE targets 85% accessibility on course shells using the built-in Canvas checker, and student assistants check tags and auto-tag PDFs. DPRC has referred two faculty whose captioning needs it could not meet. The expanded Verbit contract is in progress. The service covers captions only. No audio description capability exists.

### 5.12-ins — Initiated
The Accessible Media Quick Converter provides automated document conversion through SensusAccess. DPRC remediates print materials as a direct accommodation, not as a universal design measure. Kevin Connor and Daniel Fontaine implement. No universal design process exists for instructor-developed course readers.

### 5.13-ins — Defined
AT's Canvas remediation process addresses document accessibility on a rolling basis, starting with sections over 100 students. Kevin Connor runs day-to-day remediation (in his position description) with six student workers. PDF work began in summer 2026 using a two-pass approach: Adobe Acrobat first, Equidox for anything Acrobat cannot handle. Cross-campus comparison with Sonoma confirmed the same process at different scale. In fall 2026 AT started a UDOIT-based files queue that bulk-downloads, remediates, and re-uploads flagged files. A student built a tool routing UDOIT-flagged files to the Adobe PDF Services API for automated remediation with manual review; the tool is in development and has run on test courses. The Accessible Media Quick Converter provides SensusAccess conversion. Most flagged files are PDFs accumulated over years. Whiteboard images and math formulas have no remediation path.

### 5.14-ins — Initiated
CEETL's Excellence in Online Pedagogies course covers emerging technology accessibility, adapting the Quality Matters Bridge to Quality guide. Angie Petty implements. No other process or implementation addresses this indicator. The evidence is limited to professional development content.

### 5.15-ins — Initiated
The only wired implementation (SF State Accessible Technology Procurement Process) is retired. Daniel Fontaine implements. Faculty request publisher tools through the bookstore, which bypasses procurement. AT moved LTI installs into BuyIT over several years, but BuyIT's retirement removes that control. A plan for closer DPRC/AT collaboration was abandoned. Procurement reviews a basic VPAT at acquisition. DPRC remediation remains accommodation-only.

### 5.16-ins — Defined
AT routes every LTI request through BuyIT for accessibility review, operated by Andrew Roderick, Mantasha Lakdawala, Cristian Alvarado, and Daniel Fontaine. The BuyIT/TAAP process includes a checkbox for course-related purchases that triggers a more detailed review. DesignPLUS was piloted over summer 2026 with 12 faculty who each redesigned at least one fall course. Pilot faculty recorded showcase videos published on the DesignPLUS webpage. AT is running a longitudinal evaluation with faculty sponsor Chris Bettinger comparing DesignPLUS and non-DesignPLUS courses. DesignPLUS includes an accessibility checker that flags issues inside the design flow. The CSU is considering DesignPLUS for its standard licensed tool package. BuyIT is the only step that puts an LTI request in front of accessibility review, and no replacement has been named for its retirement.


## Goal 6: Course Accessibility

### 6.2-ins — Initiated
The campus course syllabus policy references DPRC but does not link to accessibility information. The campus course review and approval process refers to accessibility requirements but does not include a link to the course accessibility checklist on the page itself. JEDI PIE addresses accessible course materials as part of its disability justice certificate. No formal route exists for DPRC to notify AT that a course needs remediation; requests arrive by email. Cristian Alvarado and Christina Passmore are working out the details of a notification process, including filtering which DPRC accommodation types are relevant to digital materials. Andrew Roderick and Daniel Fontaine implement. One implementation (Public Instructions and Checklists about Digital Course Accessibility) is retired. A plan to include accessibility information in the course approval process is on hold.

### 6.4-ins — Defined
AT and CEETL run multiple concurrent faculty development courses in the LMS across semesters, among them Excellence in Online Pedagogies, Renovate and Elevate in Canvas, and Universal Design for Learning. Each course has run for multiple semesters with AT and CEETL facilitators. AT's internal CPaGE processes target 85% accessibility on course shells using the built-in Canvas checker, with student assistants checking tags. AT does not push QLT or Quality Matters; Andrew Roderick sees UDL 3.0 as a stronger pedagogical basis. DPRC no longer has resources to support course accessibility reviews. Kevin Connor, Ash Klein, Daniel Fontaine, and Angie Petty implement. CPaGE practices operate within AT and are not campus-wide. A plan to verify accessibility information in AT/CEETL courses is on hold.

### 6.5-ins — Defined
Seven implementations address accessibility at course redesign, among them JEDI Teaching Square, the Accessible Syllabus Tool, JEDI PIE, AT's internal CPaGE processes, and DesignPLUS. DesignPLUS was piloted over summer 2026 with 12 faculty who each redesigned at least one fall course. Pilot faculty recorded showcase videos published on the DesignPLUS webpage. AT treats DesignPLUS as an emergent, roughly three-year capacity project rather than core practice. Andrew Roderick committed to producing a one-pager documenting DesignPLUS's accessibility value, but no documentation has been delivered. AT is running a longitudinal evaluation with faculty sponsor Chris Bettinger comparing DesignPLUS and non-DesignPLUS courses. The JEDI Teaching Square is co-led by Julie Paulson through a CSU Stanislaus grant focused on neurodivergence and course-redesign accessibility. CEETL funds about 8 additional participants to reach a cohort of 20. The Accessible Syllabus Tool is supported but not promoted. CPaGE practices operate within one unit. No pre- or post-redesign metrics exist. The budget element of this indicator is unmet.

### 6.7-ins — Defined
AT scanned spring 2026 Canvas courses with UDOIT and raised average accessibility scores from about 71% to the 80s by summer. Batch UDOIT scans run each term, fixing missing alt text, wrong heading levels, insufficient color contrast, and decorative text styled as headings. DPRC's Canvas Bot runs daily scans for courses where a student has requested accommodation, giving alt media staff immediate access to flagged files. Cristian Alvarado and Daniel Fontaine implement, and Kevin Connor with six student workers staff the remediation the evaluations trigger. No manual evaluation method exists. No written scan schedule is on record. SF State's longstanding accommodation-triggered course review, where enrollment by a student with an accommodation triggers an accessibility review, is not currently operating.

### 6.8-ins — Defined
Kevin Connor runs day-to-day Canvas remediation with the work in his position description. Six student workers help, three doing most of the work at the help desk between customers. AT inherited Canvas remediation in spring 2026 and runs batch UDOIT scans, random student-designer assignment, mail-merge faculty notifications, and rescans on completion. The spreadsheet of record feeds per-department reports for chairs and deans. Spring 2026 covered about 4,000 courses and summer 862. By October 2026, 21.7% of ATI points had been removed. Post-remediation UDOIT scores trend in the mid-to-high 80s. PDF remediation has run on test courses only. The Accessible Media Quick Converter handles format conversion. AT is pursuing the CSUEB AI document remediation platform at the SFBRN level rather than hosting it at SF State alone. Whiteboard images and math formulas have no remediation path. A plan to maintain a shared ATI materials repository is on hold.

### 6.9-ins — Initiated
One TAAP exists (Pearson Revel). The SFBRN CSUBuy IT Accessibility Review Procedure operates with documented cross-campus queue rules, a 30-day pickup SLA for unassigned reviews, reviewer triage heuristics, standard communication templates, and a rule of three for unresponsive requesters. People are directed to BuyIT for LTI accessibility review, but no campus policy requires publisher tools to go through BuyIT. Faculty request publisher tools through the bookstore, which bypasses the review entirely. The retired SF State Accessible Technology Procurement Process has no active replacement. No Equally Effective Alternate Access Plan exists for the LMS or its integrations. A plan for campus-wide TAAP implementation including ITS, DPRC, HR, and Colleges is not started. A plan for clearer EEAAP instructions for LTI is on hold.


## Goal 7: Faculty Responsibility and Resources

### 7.1-ins — Initiated
Three implementations touch this indicator: the Affordable Instructional Materials Initiative, the campus course syllabus policies, and the SF State Academic Continuity Plan. The syllabus policy references DPRC but does not address faculty responsibility for selecting or authoring accessible materials. Faculty receive encouragement through the website, orientation, new course proposals, and the AIM program. Andrew Roderick implements. No formal policy, resolution, or procedure documents faculty responsibility for accessible instructional materials. A plan for a senate policy rooted in the Title II update was abandoned. No one in Academic Affairs has been assigned ownership.

### 7.3-ins — Initiated
Five implementations provide tools and guidance adjacent to this indicator: the Accessible Media Quick Converter, the Accessible Syllabus Tool, CEETL courses, the DesignPLUS rollout, and public Canvas accessibility guidance. None contains what the indicator asks for: actual examples of accessible instructional materials. No process exists to create, distribute, or update such examples. The Accessible Syllabus Tool is supported but not promoted. AT treats DesignPLUS as an emergent, roughly three-year project with no written documentation. The DesignPLUS pilot with 12 faculty produced showcase videos of redesigned courses. The SFBRN ATI website is proposed to host examples in a future phase. A plan for CEETL to publish course-redesign accessibility guidance to YouTube is not started. Cristian Alvarado, Daniel Fontaine, and Kevin Connor implement.

### 7.5-ins — Defined
Eight active implementations provide resources for creating accessible instructional content: JEDI PIE, New Faculty Foundation orientation, AT's Canvas remediation process, CEETL courses, the Accessible Syllabus Tool, the Accessible Media Quick Converter, public Canvas accessibility guidance, and CPaGE processes. CPaGE maintains four job aids and targets 85% accessibility on course shells. AT's remediation program covers about 4,000 courses per term with UDOIT scores trending in the mid-to-high 80s. Kevin Connor runs day-to-day remediation with six student workers. Andrew Roderick, Anoshua Chaudhuri, Ash Klein, Cristian Alvarado, and Daniel Fontaine implement, but only two of eight implementations have an assigned owner. Three retired guidance implementations are still linked as evidence. SF State's accessibility page is planned as the central hub, and AT is building a faculty-facing documentation catalog (in progress, Andrew Roderick owns). No single documented pathway ties the resources together for a faculty member. No output evidence or metrics exist.

### 7.11-ins — Defined
Six active implementations address library accessibility across the asset lifecycle. Electronic theses and dissertations go through a staged process with templates and quality control before publication in ScholarWorks. The Bay Area Television Archives captioning project is roughly 80% complete against about 3,000 clips, with completion expected ahead of the April 2027 deadline. The electronic resource acquisitions procedure requires vendor accessibility documentation before purchase, with VPATs kept on file. The Library does not independently review VPAT claims; a centralized review at the Chancellor's Office is in development. When a patron reports an accessibility problem, the request arrives through Library chat or email and is routed to Ya Wang's Electronic Resources team, who contact the vendor directly. The SFBRN CSUBuy IT Accessibility Review Procedure handles procurement-level accessibility review with documented queue rules, SLAs, and communication templates. Christy Stevens, Matthew James Martin, Michael McCourt, and Ya Wang implement with no position-description flags. The intake triage runs on convention rather than written procedure, and requests are not tagged or counted. An accessibility LibGuide exists but needs a refresh.


## Goal 8: Communication, Training and Professional Development

### 8.1-ins — Defined
AT's Canvas remediation process, CEETL courses, the administrative review process and JEDI PIE all carry accessibility messaging. DPRC has conducted presentations and sits on committees. No formal communication campaign with executive support exists. Faculty respond better to evidence-based rationale than compliance mandates alone. CSU WebAIM training was communicated in a campus memo, but the effort is episodic.

### 8.3-ins — Defined
Four implementations provide training materials, among them JEDI Teaching Square, CEETL courses, public Canvas accessibility guidance and JEDI PIE. AT plans to release online tutorials and videos in fall 2026 for faculty self-service. AT currently does the bulk of remediation work. The intended shift toward shared responsibility with faculty has not been documented. No formal process document exists. The selecting and adopting verbs in the indicator have zero evidence.

### 8.11-ins — Defined
All new faculty attend a UDL session during the New Faculty Foundation orientation. In 2026, Julie Paulson delivered UDL content and AT instructional designers covered digital accessibility. DPRC greeted attendees and gave a tour but delivered no structured presentation, a change from prior years. No attendance records are on file. No one owns this implementation.

### 8.12-ins — Defined
Five implementations address faculty development, among them JEDI Teaching Square, New Faculty Foundation orientation, CEETL courses and JEDI PIE. CEETL's 2026-2027 catalog names disability justice. About 50 faculty have completed the JEDI certificate series. AT, CEETL and DPRC collaborate, but no unit is named as accountable for accessibility in faculty development. CEETL stipends ended, and no accessibility-specific role remains at CEETL. The attested development process is not documented, and the associated plan is on hold.

### 8.13-ins — Initiated
AT reported ATI progress to the Academic Technology Advisory Committee, covering Canvas remediation, the files queue and the DesignPLUS accessibility checker. The Canvas LTI accessibility gate via BuyIT and CEETL courses also address this indicator. Ad hoc approaches are in place but lack formal documentation.

### 8.14-ins — Initiated
Various free professional development materials are available. No budget supports ongoing PD for employees with ATI instructional materials responsibilities. Student-assistant training and DesignPLUS training may already constitute PD for ATI employees, but neither is documented as such. The associated plan is on hold.
