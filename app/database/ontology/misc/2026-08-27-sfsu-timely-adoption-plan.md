## Plan for 1.1-ins, 1.2-ins, 1.6-ins · AY 2026-2027

Prepared 2026-08-27 · Instructional Materials working group · SFSU

**Sources for this plan:** the message record from May–August 2026, and the success
indicators' own companion guides (the authored Established bars for 1.2 and 1.6; 1.1 has

## 1. Where things stand

The three indicators are one practice. 1.1 is the **authority** — a formally documented
process. 1.2 is the **machine** — staff, timelines, training, tracking. 1.6 is the
**feedback loop** — per-semester performance reports to administration. None of them can
be finished alone, and right now all three are waiting on the same decision.

**The sequence in the record:**

**May 2026 — the process is designed.** The Bookstore Advisory Committee subcommittee
works out the flow: Chancellor's Office letter → Provost's Office → Deans → Department
Chairs → Faculty, with Follett feeding adoption data back through the chain. Andrew
Roderick asks that department chairs be named explicitly at steps 2 and 3, since chairs
are who actually drive the work. Kathleen Hayes (Follett) offers reporting at any
cadence, including a leaderboard, and warns about disintermediation — faculty sending
students straight to publishers, which compromises student data privacy and blocks
financial aid use. Sara Meeks raises that students on financial aid and VA benefits are
blocked outright when adoptions never reach the bookstore. Chris Farmer notes the
adoption data also feeds the class schedule's no-cost/low-cost designations, and the
library depends on it. Rob Strong recalls the incentive scheme that worked historically —
$500 to chairs and $5,000 to deans for departments hitting 90% timely adoption. Next
step recorded: Brian Beatty revises the document, returns it to the committee, then takes
it to the Provost's Office.

**Spring 2026 — the recommendation is approved.** UCorp and its committee reconstructed
the requirement, documented how it was met in the past, and laid out a step-by-step
implementation. The BAC approves it. Chris Farmer's framing: the campus is required to
have this in place, the CO monitors it for every campus, UCorp has been happy to assist
but this is not UCorp's to own — "the most appropriate area for this is within Academic
Affairs."

**2026-07-30 — the vendor moves first.** Andrea Rion (Follett) emails seven deans
directly: over 1,600 Fall 2026 sections still have no course material adoption, under 30
days from the first day of classes. The message cites HEOA textbook pricing disclosure,
names the student harms — limited affordable purchasing options, reduced inventory,
blocked equitable access programs, students without materials in the first weeks — and
points faculty to Follett Discover through MySFSU. It even asks faculty to mark "No
Course Materials Required" so the record is complete.

**2026-07-31 — Chris Farmer reads it as validation.** It is close to what the committee
designed, and the deans had already signalled support for exactly this pattern when he
and Tammie Ridgell met the Provost's Council a year earlier. But it is the first one he
has seen from Follett, and the difference that matters is direction: **the messaging
should come from the campus to the deans, then out through the colleges with chairs and
AOCs supporting — not from the bookstore.**

**2026-08-04 — the recommendation is still parked.** Chris checks in with Andrew,
having held off waiting for the Provost's return.

**Andrew's reply — a named path opens.** Mona raised the earlier message at convocation
and suggested a presentation to Deans' Council as a good start. Andrew's advice: Chris
should go to Mona directly with a brief background, the specific ask for the Provost's
Office, and the offer of that presentation, cc'ing Andrew.

**What this means for the plan.** The analysis is done. The process is designed. The
document is drafted and approved. There is no more committee work to do. The entire
programme is blocked on one decision inside Academic Affairs, and as of this month there
is a named person to ask and a named venue to ask in. Everything below is sequenced
behind that.

## 2. What the bar actually requires

Taken from the indicators' companion guides, element by element, with who has to produce
each one. This is the specification the plan is built to satisfy.

### 1.1-ins — formally documented process
*"Campus has formally documented (e.g., Policy, Resolution, or Procedure) a process to
ensure the timely adoption of textbooks and other instructional materials."*

No companion guide is authored for this indicator, so the generic rubric governs:
**Defined** wants a working draft stored in a campus electronic location; **Established**
wants documentation that is complete, reflects the standard practice, is stored campus-side
and **has been communicated to the campus**, with resources allocated and ATI tasks
written into job descriptions.

The decisive point for this plan: since the 2008 policy is being replaced rather than
revived, 1.1 is satisfied by **the new policy or procedure**, ratified and published —
not by pointing at what already exists.

### 1.2-ins — the process that achieves compliance

| Established element (companion guide) | What has to exist | Who produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Written campuswide process defining how and when materials are adopted | The BAC-approved recommendation, adopted and published as campus procedure | Academic Affairs owner |
| …accounting for late-added instructors by auto-adopting textbooks | A written auto-adopt rule for previously-taught sections, plus the late-hire path | Chris Farmer + Follett |
| Position description | The adoption cycle written into a real position's duties | Provost's Office + HR |
| Budget | Named funding for coordination and accessibility review | Academic Affairs |
| Roles assigned across departments, library, instructional design, accessibility, ATI, bookstore | A RACI inside the procedure, naming chairs and AOCs explicitly per Andrew's May request | Academic Affairs owner |
| Faculty informed of timelines allowing accessibility review before instruction | Published adoption calendar with remediation lead time built in | Academic Affairs + DPRC |
| Staff time explicitly allocated | Named FTE or fraction for adoption coordination | Academic Affairs |
| Training and guidance regularly provided to faculty | Faculty-facing guidance and reminder templates; Follett's virtual office hours folded in | ATI + Follett |
| Technology or tracking to monitor status and flag risk early | Follett Discover plus a campus-side view of who is outstanding | ATI + Follett |
| Used consistently across terms, reviewed periodically | Two consecutive clean cycles | Academic Affairs owner |
| Consistent Provost / Dean / Chair communication | The memo cascade, issued every term on schedule | Provost's Office |

Evidence the guide asks for, restated as artifacts to produce: the documented workflow
with roles and decision points; written deadline guidance for faculty and departments;
defined staff roles; the staff-time allocation; training materials; the tracking system;
the reminder communications themselves; and adoption-timeline statistics from the
bookstore platform.

### 1.6-ins — semester performance reports

| Established element (companion guide) | What has to exist | Who produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Reports generated on a predictable schedule aligned with terms | A fixed reporting calendar — two per term | Academic Affairs owner |
| Reports include meaningful metrics on timely adoption and accessibility readiness | An agreed metric set, not a raw missing-sections dump | ATI + Follett |
| Administration receives them in time to act | Delivery pegged to deadline−30, not after the fact | Academic Affairs owner |
| Responsibilities for creation, review and distribution clearly defined | Named producer, named reviewer, fixed distribution list | Academic Affairs owner |
| Reports used to inform planning, resourcing and improvement | A standing agenda item where findings produce actions | Deans' Council |

Evidence the guide asks for: the documented reporting schedule; a standard report
template or dashboard; the defined roles; the semester reports as actually sent to deans
and the provost's office; the bookstore/LMS data behind them; meeting materials showing
administration reviewed them; and documentation of follow-up actions taken.

**Note the gap this exposes.** Follett's July email is a missing-sections list, not a
performance report. A performance report says *how the campus is doing* — compliance rate
by college, trend against last term, how many sections are still at risk with N days to
go. Kathleen Hayes has already offered to produce exactly that, at any cadence, including
a per-college leaderboard. That offer is unspent.

## 4. Sequencing against the adoption calendar

SF State's published deadlines are **mid-October for Spring** and **mid-April for Fall**
(confirm the exact 2026-27 dates with Academic Affairs before committing to them). That
sets the whole rhythm:

| When | What has to be true |
|---|---|
| **Sept 2026** | A1 sent. Deans' Council slot requested. |
| **Sept–Oct 2026** | A2 presented. A3 decided. |
| **Mid-Oct 2026 — Spring 2027 deadline** | First campus-issued reminder goes out, even if only as the one-off fallback. |
| **Nov–Dec 2026** | C1–C3 agreed. First performance report produced for the Spring 2027 cycle. B1–B5 drafted. |
| **Spring 2027** | D1–D3 through ratification. Procedure and calendar published. Deans' Council review becomes standing. |
| **Mid-Apr 2027 — Fall 2027 deadline** | First full cycle under the new process: memo cascade, tracking, deadline−30 report. |
| **May–Jun 2027** | Second report. Cycle reviewed for effectiveness. SFBRN share. |

Fall 2026 is not recoverable — the deadline passed in April and the 1,600 outstanding
sections are being chased now. **Spring 2027 is the first cycle SF State can actually
run**, which is what makes the September window matter.

## 6. Open questions to settle

1. **Who is the owner?** Academic Affairs is the right home per the committee's finding,
   but no position has been named. The historical precedent Chris cites is what Provost
   Summit and AVP Gene Chelberg ran in prior years — the modern equivalent has to be
   identified before A3 can be answered.
2. **Which vehicle for 1.1** — Senate policy, revision, or Provost's directive? This
   determines whether ratification takes a semester or a month.
3. **Does the CO memo arrive on a schedule the campus can plan around?** The cascade
   design assumes it triggers the cycle. If its timing is unreliable, the campus memo
   should be scheduled independently and reference the requirement directly.
4. **Are incentives on the table?** The historical $500/$5,000 scheme reportedly moved
   departments to 90%. Worth asking whether anything comparable is fundable, because the
   plan otherwise relies entirely on communication.
5. **What is being done about disintermediation?** Faculty pointing students directly at
   publishers defeats adoption tracking, financial aid use and student data privacy at
   once. It sits outside the adoption cascade and nobody owns it yet.
