Since July 2026 all three campuses also run reviews through the shared SFBRN procedure, which has its own rules.

What a shared SFBRN procedure needs

  • One vocabulary. Agree what “impact” and “risk” mean, and use the same words in the procedure, the intake form, campus pages and TAAPs. We review the vendor’s ACR; the VPAT is only the blank template.
  • Use and evidence kept apart. Rate how much a product matters from how it will be used. Judge the vendor’s ACR separately. A good ACR should not reduce how closely we check it.
  • Rules in plain numbers. One user threshold for all three campuses, and one rule for what makes a product High. One serious factor should be enough.
  • The best questions from each campus. SF State: could it affect a student’s grade, and is it behind single sign-on. East Bay: does it publish content, and is it required for employment or for coursework. SFBRN: is the product itself an accommodation.
  • Every way of buying. P2P requisitions, P-cards, auxiliaries, free tools, LTI integrations, products students must buy, and systemwide contracts go through the same review.
  • A record of every review, not only TAAPs. The answers, the level, the reason and the reviewer, so decisions can be counted, compared across campuses and defended later.
  • Intake that asks what the review needs. The purchase request should collect the facts the procedure uses, so reviewers are not rebuilding them from comments.
  • Decisions that expire. A review is redone at renewal, when users or scope grow, or after a year.
  • One home for the procedure. Keep it in one place and retire old campus pages. Some SF State pages still send requesters to DPRC.
  • Tested before adoption. Run past requests from each campus, including the 27 TAAPs, through it and look at where the answers differ.
  • One standard. WCAG 2.1 Level AA, as agreed on 10/14.
  • Four outcomes, decided by the matrix. Approve, manual review, TAAP or deny. Manual review returns a recommendation to the reviewer who owns the request. The matrix should also say when a barrier is too serious for a TAAP to cover, so deny is a defensible outcome rather than a hot potato.
  • A clear TAAP threshold. A TAAP when a barrier stops someone completing a task, not when a product is merely awkward. A working alternative the vendor offers counts against a TAAP. Judge barriers by who meets them: a faculty-only screen is not the student path.
  • Answers we can check. Some requesters report “one user” for classroom tools. Rely on facts the reviewer can confirm, such as license count, course use and single sign-on, and keep both the requester’s answer and the reviewer’s.
  • Every content type. Software, web pages, public-facing electronic content, official communications and non-web documents. The current tool covers software only.
  • More than the ACR. ATI team testing and live vendor demonstrations as recognised evidence, with “Partially Supports” given an agreed meaning.
  • Look back. Re-review past TAAP decisions so reviewers converge, and check that issued TAAPs work in practice. No complaints is not proof that an alternative works.

  • Content type first: software, web page, public-facing electronic content, official communication, or non-web document. The type decides which accessibility rules apply in Step 2.
  • The highest level in any row is the impact level. One High row makes the product High.
  • Use facts the reviewer can confirm: license count on the requisition, course listings, whether access is behind single sign-on. Record the requester’s answer and the reviewer’s answer.
  • An answer nobody can give is rated at the higher plausible level until it is answered.
  • A reviewer may raise a level with a reason. Nobody lowers it without a second approver.
  • A product bought as one person’s accommodation goes to the accommodation process instead.

Step 2. Barrier severity: what the evidence shows, judged against WCAG 2.1 AA

Level Meaning
S0 Supports every WCAG 2.1 AA criterion that applies
S1 Barriers only in features nobody needs to complete their task
S2 An essential function is harder but can still be completed independently, or the vendor offers a working alternative channel that completes the same task
S3 An essential function cannot be completed by at least one disability group, and no working alternative channel exists
S? Unknown: no ACR, demonstration or testing
  • Judge severity for each kind of user separately (students, employees, the public). Use the severity on the path of the people who drive the impact level. A barrier on a faculty-only screen does not set the severity of the student path.
  • Evidence, weakest to strongest: E0 none; E1 the vendor’s ACR; E2 the ACR checked against a live vendor demonstration or a third-party audit; E3 ATI team testing.
  • “Partially Supports” is S1 or S2 depending on whether the criterion touches an essential function. It is never S0.
