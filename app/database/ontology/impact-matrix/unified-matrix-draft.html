DRAFT: Unified SFBRN impact matrix, version 2

For discussion. Version 2, 16 October 2026. Rewritten from the Procurement Working Group meeting of 10/14/2026 and a review of the law, enforcement history, federal practice and peer universities. Every rule shows its decision state.

Decision states

State Meaning
Agreed Decided by the Procurement Working Group on 10/14/2026.
Required Follows from law or CSU policy. The matrix cannot choose otherwise. The source is named.
Proposed A design choice recommended from the research. The working group has not decided it.
Calibrate A number or term the pilot will set. No law or principle picks it.

The reviewer records what is being bought before rating anything. The kind of technology sets the standard.

A product with both, such as a desktop client and a web portal WCAG 2.1 AA for the web and mobile use As for web Required

Each interface (public, student, instructor, administrative) and each platform (web, iOS, Android) is reviewed on its own, with its own ACR. Required
An alternate version of web content is acceptable only where direct access is not possible for technical or legal reasons (28 CFR 35.202). Required

Step 1. Exposure: how widely and how necessarily the product is used

Renamed from “impact”, because in the Title II rule “impact” means the effect of a barrier on a person. Exposure sets how deep the review goes, how strong the evidence must be, who approves, and how long a decision lasts. It never decides whether access is owed. Title II and Section 504 have no threshold of users below which access is optional, and OCR applies the duty to pilots and to classes with no student known to have a disability. (Required)

Floors: one fact sets a minimum level

Floors apply first and only raise the level.

Fact Minimum State
Required of students for coursework or a grade High Proposed
The only route to a university service, such as enrollment, financial aid, payment or housing High Proposed
Campus-wide, or all sections of a course in a curriculum High Proposed
A person with a disability is known to be in the user group, or an accommodation request about the product is open High Proposed
A prior accessibility complaint about the product High Proposed
Required for a job duty, or used in hiring (an applicant portal counts as public) Medium Proposed
Open to the public without single sign-on, publishes content to the public, or sits in a public space such as an open kiosk Medium Proposed

Knowing that no one with a disability is in the user group never lowers the level. (Required)

Rule State
Factors are merged into four groups so no fact is counted twice. Proposed
User cut points of 25 and 250. The previous draft used 5 and 25, far below every peer model. The required-use floors catch small required cases. Calibrate
Groups score Low 0, Medium 1, High 2. The weighted total is read as a percent of the possible total. Proposed
Bands: Low under 25 percent, Medium 25 to 55 percent, High 55 percent and above. Group weights set with reviewers from each campus. Calibrate
A simple decision table runs beside the points in the pilot (High if any floor applies or two groups are High). The one that agrees better with an expert panel is kept. Proposed
No likelihood axis. Risk-matrix research shows impact-by-likelihood grids misrank risks. Proposed
The requester's and the reviewer's answers are both recorded. A reviewer may raise a level with a reason. Lowering needs a second approver. Proposed
An answer nobody can give is scored both ways. If the results differ, the outcome is “request information”. Unknowns are rated high because the university carries the burden of proof. Proposed; Required
A product bought for one person's accommodation goes to the accommodation process. Proposed
Left out of exposure: negative points for Low answers, data sensitivity (security review) and cost (high cost alone is not a basis for an exception). Proposed

Step 2. Barrier severity: what a person can do

Judged with the Title II tests (28 CFR 35.205): the same information, interactions and transactions, with substantially equivalent timeliness, privacy, independence and ease of use. The WCAG criterion is the evidence, not the verdict. (Required)

Level Definition GSA label
S0 No effect on information, interactions, transactions or participation. none
S1 Minimal impact, demonstrated: the four Title II tests are documented as met. Minor
S2 A core task can be completed independently, with clearly unequal time or effort. Major
S3 A person in that role cannot complete a core task independently, or only with another person's help. Critical
Rule State
The TAAP threshold is a barrier that blocks a task: S3. Agreed
A vendor's alternative channel weighs against a TAAP, but only when it is equally effective: same task, same time, independent and private. A channel where staff do the task for the person is assistance and leaves the barrier at S3. Agreed, narrowed by law
An alternative method inside the product that gives equal or greater access can support S0 or S1 (equivalent facilitation, 28 CFR 35.203). Required
“Not a core feature” is not enough for S1. S1 needs the documented minimal-impact finding, because the university carries that burden. Required
Severity is rated per role (students, employees, applicants, the public) and per disability group. The worst cell sets the role's severity. Proposed
Core tasks are listed per role before testing, and each is judged as a complete process. Proposed
Keyboard traps, flashing content, auto-playing audio and moving content that cannot be stopped are at least S2 anywhere, and S3 when they trap or endanger the user (WCAG non-interference). Required
“Partially Supports” is never S0. Proposed

Evidence

“ACR”, not “VPAT”. ATI team testing and live vendor demonstrations count as evidence. (Agreed)

Level What it holds State
E0 Nothing, or marketing statements Proposed
E1- A vendor ACR that fails a quality check Proposed
E1 A vendor ACR that passes every quality check Proposed
E1+ E1 plus GSA's issue-level supplement: severity, affected group, workaround Proposed
E2a A live vendor demonstration of each role's core tasks with assistive technology Agreed
E2b A third-party audit that names its method, such as Trusted Tester Proposed
E3 ATI team testing of each role's core tasks by a named method Agreed

An ACR drops to E1- if: it reports only WCAG 2.0 for a web or mobile product; it has no date or version, or the wrong version; it is older than two years (Calibrate) or the last major release; it names no test methods; “Partially Supports” or “Does Not Support” has no remarks; it says only “Supports” throughout; it uses “Not Evaluated” outside AAA; or it misses an interface or platform being bought. (Proposed)

Evidence limits the outcome: E0 or E1- never supports Approve at Medium or High exposure; E1 never supports Approve at High; an S1 finding needs E2 or better. (Proposed)

Step 3. Outcome

The four agreed outcomes are approve, manual review, TAAP and deny (Agreed). Approve has two forms in this draft.

Outcome Meaning State
Approve No known barrier on any role's path. Agreed
Approve with barrier record Known S1 or S2 barriers. Users are told where they meet the product; the vendor's fix dates go in the contract; a review date is set. This meets indicator 4.6-pro's call for a plan whenever a product with known barriers is approved, without stretching the TAAP. Proposed
TAAP S3 on a core task, with an alternative that passes every TAAP test below. Agreed
Deny S3 where a TAAP cannot pass its tests, or a more accessible product meets the need. Agreed
Manual review Routes uncertainty, not a barrier: unknown severity, weak evidence, a borderline score, or a claimed exception. It returns a recommendation; the reviewer who owns the requisition decides. Agreed; Proposed

Step 4. What every decision records

Every decision is recorded, including low-exposure approvals, so indicators 1.3-pro, 1.12-pro, 8.9-pro and 8.10-pro can be met from the records. (Required for the indicator bars; the list is Proposed.)

  • Kind of technology, interfaces and platforms, and the standard that applies.
  • Each exposure answer as the requester gave it and as the reviewer confirmed it; any floor that applied; the group scores; the number of unknowns.
  • Core tasks per role; severity per role and disability group.
  • Evidence level; ACR date, edition and version; any quality checks it failed.
  • Products compared, and the best-meets basis where used.
  • Outcome; contract terms; approver's name and title; date.
  • Expiry date and what triggers a new decision.
  • The matrix version used.

What changed from version 1

  • “Impact” became “exposure”, and exposure no longer decides an outcome on its own.
  • A Step 0 picks the standard by kind of technology.
  • “Highest level wins” became floors first, then four merged groups.
  • User cut points moved from 5 and 25 to 25 and 250, to be calibrated.
  • Severity is defined by the Title II tests; “unknown” left the grid and became a routing rule.
  • A vendor's alternative channel counts only when it is equally effective.
  • The evidence ladder gained levels and eight ACR quality checks.
  • Approve gained a second form, the barrier record. S3 is never Approve.
  • TAAP or deny turns on whether the plan can serve every affected person from the first day, and whether a more accessible product exists.
  • Approver and expiry scale with exposure. Contract terms follow the outcome.
