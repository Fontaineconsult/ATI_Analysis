# SFBRN review rubric, core model

This file states the SFBRN accessibility review rubric, matrix version 2, as formulas, and
nothing else. `review-matrix.js` implements the rubric. `rubric-core.svg` renders the rubric
as one decision table, regenerated by `build-rubric-table.js` after any constant change.
`review-process.svg` renders the decision path as one flowchart, regenerated by
`build-process-diagram.js` after any hold or outcome change. `review-process-checks.svg`
and `review-process-remedy.svg` render the same path as two page-width figures for the
rubric document, regenerated by `build-process-figures.js`. `build-rubric-docx.py` builds
`SFBRN-Accessible-Procurement-Rubric.docx` from the tester's definitions, this file's
overview and the three figures. The `AUTHORITY` table in `review-matrix.js` cites the law
and policy behind each final decision.

## Terms

### Review standard

- **Review standard.** The standard each review tests one campus-requested product against,
  WCAG 2.1 AA for web and mobile products or the Section 508 Standards for other software and
  hardware.

### Essential function

- **Essential function.** A function the product exists to provide, such as registering for a
  class, submitting homework or paying a bill.
  - **Test.** A function is essential when its removal would change the product's use or
    purpose. WCAG-EM 2.0 defines essential functionality by the same test.
  - **Source.** The requester lists the minimum functional requirements, which CSU
    Procurement Step 1 calls "essential functionality", and the reviewer confirms the list.
  - **Scope.** An essential function covers its complete process, including sign-in, navigation, the
    content read along the way and the final submission. WCAG 2.1 requires every page in a
    complete process to conform.
  - **Other names.** WCAG-EM says "essential functionality". CSU TAAP documents say
    "essential functions" and "core functionality". The "essential functions" of a job under
    ADA Title I (29 CFR 1630.2(n)) are a separate concept and never set severity.

### Nonconformance and barrier

The rubric separates a failure in the product from its effect on users.

- **Nonconformance.** A failed success criterion or provision of the review standard. A
  nonconformance belongs to the product.
- **Barrier.** The effect of a nonconformance on one essential function for one of the nine Section 508
  **functional groups**, such as users without vision. One nonconformance produces a barrier on
  every affected **function-group pair**. CSU procurement documents say "gap" or "issue" for a
  barrier.

### Minimal impact

- **Minimal impact.** A nonconformance has minimal impact only when people with disabilities can
  still do all four of the following with substantially equivalent timeliness, privacy,
  independence and ease of use (28 CFR 35.205). The rubric calls these the **four
  minimal-impact tests**.
  - Access the same information as people without disabilities.
  - Engage in the same interactions as people without disabilities.
  - Conduct the same transactions as people without disabilities.
  - Participate in or benefit from the same services, programs and activities as people without
    disabilities.
- **Burden.** The university must demonstrate that all four tests are met, so minimal impact
  rests on evidence, never on assumption.
- **Standard.** The tests come from the Title II web rule. The rubric applies them to non-web
  products as policy, since the Section 508 Standards have no minimal-impact clause.

### Finding

- **Finding.** The record of one barrier by user **role** (students, employees, applicants or
  the public), functional group, essential function and severity.
- **ACR lead.** A "Partially Supports" or "Does Not Support" row in the vendor's **ACR**
  (Accessibility Conformance Report, the vendor's completion of the industry VPAT template).
  A lead becomes a finding once its remark names the affected feature and the reviewer places
  that feature on an essential function.

### The four factors

- **Exposure.** Audience size and product reliance, rated low, medium or high.
- **Severity.** Impact of the worst barrier on an essential function, rated S0 to S3. Severity attaches to the
  barrier and never to the nonconformance, because the level of the failed criterion (A or AA)
  does not predict the effect on users. S1, S2 and S3 match the GSA severity terms **minor**,
  **major** and **critical**. S3 (accumulated) marks S2 barriers that add up to S3 by stacking or spread,
  and S3 (provisional) marks the fallback.
- **Breadth.** Systemic barrier reach across function-group pairs, rated 0 to 2.
- **Veracity.** Reliability of the evidence behind the findings, rated 0 to 5, with a
  **Veracity floor**, the minimum rating at which Severity is confirmed, set by Exposure and
  Severity. Below the floor the ATI review team raises the evidence. When the evidence cannot
  be raised, the review proceeds under the **fallback**, Severity S3 (provisional) with the
  barriers found as a minimum.

### The plan

- **TAAP.** Temporary Alternate Access Plan, the CSU's written plan for equally effective
  access during vendor remediation. A TAAP is held to the six requirements of the CSU TAAP
  template. A TAAP is a time-bound alternative and never makes the product conformant.

## Accessible Procurement Process Overview

A review begins when a campus unit, the **requesting unit**, submits a requisition for a
product. The review ends in one of three **final decisions**, Approve, Approve with TAAP or
Deny. Where a fact the review needs is missing, the review pauses in a **hold**, named for
what it waits for, until a named party supplies that fact. The nine steps below run in the
order the reviewer takes them. Section 3 defines each term they use.

### Read the requisition

The requisition names the product, the vendor, the quantity, the requesting unit and the
intended use. The reviewer reads four things from it.

- **Kind of technology.** Web and mobile, or other software and hardware. The kind selects
  the **review standard**, because the law names a different standard for each.
- **Exposure answers.** Who will use the product and how many, whether use is required for
  coursework, a job duty or a university service, whether the product publishes to the
  public, how often it is used and how long the contract runs.
- **Essential functions.** The requester's minimum functional requirements name them.
  Severity is rated on these functions, because a barrier matters by what it stops the
  user doing.
- **Accommodation purchase.** A product bought as an accommodation for one named person
  with a disability. The interactive process between that person and the campus settled
  what the person needs, so the reviewer approves the purchase at intake and rates nothing.
  The approval is limited to that accommodation.

A renewal is a new review of the product as it now stands, because the product, its
audience and its evidence change between contracts.

### Check for prior reviews, plans and complaints

Before rating anything, the reviewer reads what the campuses already hold on the product.

- **An earlier review or TAAP** for the same product at any of the three campuses is the
  starting point for this one.
- **A prior accessibility complaint** about the product sets Exposure to high by itself,
  because a complaint is a barrier a real user has already met.

The reviewer reads the record first so that this review reaches the result the earlier one
reached on the same facts.

### Obtain the evidence

The requesting unit obtains the vendor's accessibility documentation and attaches it to the
requisition. For most purchases that is the vendor's Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR)
for every interface being bought.

- **Missing.** The review enters hold 1, for accessibility evidence. The reviewer asks the
  requesting unit for a current report from the vendor, with any open exposure questions
  in the same message so that the unit answers once.
- **On file.** The reviewer rates the evidence by its reliability, from a vendor report
  that fails a quality check up to testing by the ATI team, because a vendor's report is a
  claim about its own product. A report marked Supports on every criterion fails a quality
  check. A perfect result is as likely to mean the evaluation was not done as that no
  barrier exists.

### Rate Exposure

The reviewer rates **Exposure**, audience size and product reliance, as low, medium or
high.

- **Score.** Seven questions map into four groups, so that two questions about the same
  fact are not counted twice. The group levels aggregate into one score, so that no single
  answer sets the level by itself. Cut points partition the score range into the three
  levels, so that every reviewer reads the same level from the same answers.
- **Floor.** A single fact, such as required use for coursework or a product open to the
  public, sets a minimum level by itself, because the duty that fact carries does not
  shrink with the audience.
- **Unknown answers.** Each counts as high, so that a missing answer never lowers the
  level. When the unanswered questions alone would raise the level, the review enters hold
  2, for exposure answers, with the requesting unit.

### Rate Severity

The reviewer turns the evidence into findings and rates the worst of them.

- **Leads.** Every "Partially Supports" and "Does Not Support" row of the ACR.
- **Findings.** Each lead placed on the essential function and the disability group it
  affects, and rated from S0, no effect, to S3, the user cannot perform the function
  alone. The rating follows the effect on the user and not the level of the failed
  criterion, because the criterion level does not predict that effect.
- **Severity.** The worst finding. Enough S2 barriers stacked on one function, or spread
  across the product, raise it to S3, because a user who must pass every barrier on a
  function is blocked by the set.
- **Veracity floor.** The evidence the review requires before it proceeds. The floor rises
  with Exposure and Severity, because a finding with more consequence needs more proof.

Below the floor, the review enters hold 3, for stronger evidence. The ATI review team
raises the evidence with the requesting unit and the vendor, through a live demonstration
of the essential functions with assistive technology or its own testing, and re-rates
Severity. When the evidence cannot be raised, the review proceeds under the **fallback**,
with Severity set to S3 (provisional), because unproven conformance is treated as hiding a
blocking barrier.

### Read the risk grade

The reviewer reads the **risk grade** from the pairing of Exposure and Severity.

- **No alternate access required.** S0 and S1 at every Exposure, and S2 at low Exposure.
  The purchase is approved, and at S2 the approval records that the product still fails
  the standard.
- **Alternate access required.** S2 at medium or high Exposure, and S3 at every Exposure.
  The review continues into the remedy step.

### Write the TAAP

Where alternate access is required, the campus writes a **TAAP** (Temporary Alternate
Access Plan), the written plan for giving people with disabilities equally effective access
another way while the vendor removes the barriers. The reviewer writes it with the person
in the requesting unit who will administer and support the product, because the unit and
not the reviewer provides the support the plan promises. The meeting has four outputs.

- How the product will be used, confirmed.
- The known barriers, named.
- The alternative method of access for each barrier.
- The unit's responsibilities for supporting access.

The plan has three fixed parts.

- **Documentation.** Links to the vendor's accessibility documentation, and under the
  fallback a statement that the product's accessibility could not be evaluated.
- **Statement.** A product-specific accessibility statement, posted wherever the product
  is in use, because a remedy the affected person cannot find is not a remedy.
- **Standard.** The CSU TAAP template holds a plan to six requirements. The **TAAP
  standard**, the number of those six a plan must meet for this purchase, rises with
  Exposure and **Breadth** from three to all six, because a plan for a larger or more
  dependent audience must cover more.

Three conditions end or pause the remedy step.

- **No TAAP yet.** The review enters hold 4, for a TAAP.
- **Below the standard.** Deny, unless the president or a designee determines in writing
  that equally effective access would be an undue burden or a fundamental alteration. A
  claim of that kind enters hold 5, for a written determination.
- **More accessible product.** At S3, a product that meets the same need with fewer
  barriers is a denial by itself, because the CSU buys the most accessible product that
  meets the need.

### Record the decision

The reviewer records the final decision in the procurement system with its reasons.

- **Approve.** States the number of users it covers, so that growth beyond that number
  returns the product to review, and whom a user contacts when the product cannot be used.
- **Approve with TAAP.** States that the plan is active and when it is reviewed, and
  carries a **warning level**, low, elevated or high, that tells the signers what residual
  risk the unit accepts.
- **Deny.** States the findings, the standard applied and the path to a new review.

The department chair or manager, the dean or division vice president and the ADA
compliance officer sign the TAAP. The reviewer files it in the campus, regional and
systemwide repositories, and the purchase proceeds only then. The contract carries the CSU
accessibility terms, and at medium and high Exposure a vendor roadmap with milestones, so
that the vendor's remediation is a contractual duty.

### Keep the plan under review

The reviewer keeps an approved TAAP under review until the vendor removes the barriers.

- **Term.** A TAAP lasts twelve months or until contract renewal, whichever comes first,
  because the plan stands only while the vendor shows progress.
- **Review.** Annually at a low warning level, and at the first roadmap milestone at an
  elevated or high level. Under the fallback, the first review re-rates Severity on
  evidence at the Veracity floor.
- **Support.** A problem report from any user starts the support the plan names, with no
  accommodation request needed.
- **Renewal.** Returns the product to intake.

## 1. Inputs and symbols

The rubric maps eight inputs to the four factors and two derived values.

| Symbol | Input | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | Purchased as an accommodation for one named person with a disability | true or false |
| `F` | Floor facts that hold | any of the seven floors in section 2 |
| `a_f` | The answer to each exposure question `f` | low, medium, high or unknown, and a count for the number of users |
| `D` | The findings | each finding names a role, a functional group `g`, an essential function `fn` and a severity `s` |
| `E` | The strongest evidence on file | one of the seven evidence levels in section 5 |
| `U` | The evidence cannot be raised, recorded by the ATI review team | true or false |
| `M` | A more accessible product meets the same need | true or false |
| `T` | TAAP status, the TAAP template requirements met, and the exception claim or determination | `T.exists`, `T.meets[1..6]`, `T.exception` in {none, claimed, determined} |

| Symbol | Value | Range |
|---|---|---|
| `X` | Exposure | low, medium, high |
| `S` | Severity | S0, S1, S2, S3, or S? with no findings and no evidence; S3 (accumulated) by stacking or spread; S3 (provisional) under the fallback |
| `B` | Breadth | 0, 1, 2 |
| `V` | Veracity | 0 to 5 |
| `P` | Pressure, from Exposure and Breadth | 0 to 4 |
| `k` | TAAP standard, the minimum count of TAAP requirements a plan must meet | 3 to 6 |
| `W` | Warning index, pressure plus the gap to a full TAAP | 0 to 9 |

Levels rank `low = 0`, `medium = 1`, `high = 2`. When `A` holds, the final decision is Approve
at intake and no factor is rated. Otherwise the whole rubric fits in four lines, once no hold
in section 6 applies.

```
trusted        = S ≠ S?  ∧  V ≥ Vmin(X, S)                 section 5
S             := S3 (provisional)   if ¬trusted ∧ U         section 5, the fallback
risk_grade     = GRID[ X ][ S ]                             section 7
final_decision = REMEDY( risk_grade, S, k(P), M, T )        section 8
```

Holds 1 to 3 pause the review before the risk grade, when `¬trusted ∧ ¬U` or when unknown
answers alone set `X`. Holds 4 and 5 are the two holds `REMEDY` returns.

## 2. Exposure `X`

Exposure rates audience size and product reliance. Seven exposure questions map uniquely
across four distinct groups to prevent data duplication.

| Group `G` | Exposure questions |
|---|---|
| reach | number of users (25 or fewer is low, 26 to 250 is medium, more than 250 is high), scope of deployment |
| stakes | required use, criticality |
| spread | published content |
| duration | frequency of use, contract length |

Each group level is the group's highest answer, with unknown answers mapped to high. The
score-based Exposure level is the **band** containing the aggregate score of the group levels,
with cut points at 0.25 and 0.55 partitioning the score range into three bands.

```
G_k   = max( a_f : f ∈ G_k )                         unknown a_f → high
score = ( Σ λ_k · rank(G_k) ) / ( Σ λ_k · 2 )        λ_k = 1 for every group
band  = high    if score ≥ 0.55
        medium  if score ≥ 0.25
        low     otherwise
X     = max( band, max( min_f : f ∈ F ) )             low when F is empty
```

Exposure is the higher of the score-based level and the highest applicable **floor**, a
single-fact Exposure minimum independent of the score.

| Floor fact | Minimum Exposure |
|---|---|
| Required of students for coursework or a grade | high |
| The only route to a university service | high |
| Campus-wide, or used in all sections of a course | high |
| A person with a disability is known to be in the user group, or an accommodation request is open | high |
| A prior accessibility complaint about the product | high |
| Required for a job duty, or used in hiring | medium |
| Open to the public without single sign-on, publishes to the public, or in a public space | medium |

## 3. Severity `S`

Severity is rated on an essential function, a function the product exists to provide, such as
registering for a class, submitting homework or paying a bill. A function is essential when its
removal would change the product's use or purpose, the WCAG-EM 2.0 test, and it covers its
complete process from sign-in to final submission.

Product severity is the maximum impact on an essential function across the findings, raised by
the two accumulation conditions below, and independent of vendor report conformance labels or
the failed WCAG criterion level.

| `S` | Definition |
|---|---|
| S0 | No effect on information, interactions, transactions or participation |
| S1 | Minimal impact, shown to meet the four tests of 28 CFR 35.205 |
| S2 | An essential function can be performed independently, with clearly unequal time or effort |
| S3 | An essential function cannot be performed independently, or only with help |
| S? | Unknown, for lack of evidence |

The levels sort by the four minimal-impact tests of 28 CFR 35.205, defined under Minimal
impact. People with disabilities must still be able to do all four with substantially
equivalent timeliness, privacy, independence and ease of use.

- Access the same information.
- Engage in the same interactions.
- Conduct the same transactions.
- Participate in or benefit from the same services, programs and activities.

| Severity | Against the four tests | What the reviewer records |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | Passes all four, with no effect at all | No finding |
| S1 | Passes all four, with the impact shown to be minimal | Each test as met, on evidence at E2 or better (E2a, E2b or E3) |
| S2 | Fails at least one, since clearly unequal time or effort fails substantially equivalent ease of use | The function the barrier slows, and for whom |
| S3 | Fails outright, since the user cannot perform the function independently | The function the barrier blocks, and for whom |

```
S  = max( s_d : d ∈ D )       S0 when D is empty and E > E0
S  = S?                       when D is empty and E = E0
S := S3 (provisional)         under the fallback, section 5
```

Two accumulation conditions escalate S2 to S3, shown as "S3 (accumulated)".

| Condition | What is counted | Threshold | Exposure | Why it escalates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stacked | S2 findings on one function-group pair | 3 or more | Any | A user performs a function only by passing every barrier on it, so stacked barriers block the function |
| Spread | Distinct function-group pairs with an S2 finding | 6 or more | High only | Barriers across most of the product exceed minimal impact in aggregate |

- **Stacked.** 3 or more S2 barriers on one essential function for one functional group, at any
  Exposure. A user performs a function only by passing every barrier on it, so stacked S2
  barriers block that function. Example: a homework platform, where students who are blind
  meet 3 S2 barriers on the one function "Submit homework". The upload button has no
  accessible name, so the screen reader user finds it by trial. The due-date field accepts
  keyboard input only in an undocumented format. The confirmation is not announced, so the
  student reloads the page to check that the submission went through. Each barrier alone
  leaves the submission possible with clearly more effort, so each is S2. The student must
  pass all 3 in sequence to submit, so together they block the function, and the product is
  S3 (accumulated).
- **Spread.** S2 barriers on 6 or more function-group pairs, at high Exposure only. Barriers spread
  across most of the product exceed minimal impact in aggregate. Example: a campus-wide student
  portal at high Exposure, where no barrier blocks a function but the reviewer records S2
  findings on 6 different pairs: pay tuition for students who are blind (unlabelled form
  fields), pay tuition for students with limited use of their hands (small controls with no
  keyboard shortcut), view balance for students with low vision (3 to 1 contrast on the balance
  table), register for classes for students who are blind (search results not announced),
  register for classes for students with cognitive disabilities (a session timeout with a
  ten-second warning), and view financial aid for students who are deaf (video instructions
  with the transcript on another page). The product is S3 (accumulated).

Neither condition applies to 2 S2 findings on one pair, or to 6 pairs at medium Exposure. Those
stay S2, though 3 or more S2 findings still raise Breadth (section 4). Six S2 findings on one
pair are one pair for spread but twice the stacking threshold, so they stack. The pair, not the
finding, is the unit spread counts.

```
stacked(fn, g) = | { d : s_d = S2, function fn, group g } |
S2 pairs       = | { (fn, g) : s_d = S2 } |

S := S3   if S = S2  ∧  ( any stacked(fn, g) ≥ 3  ∨  ( X = high ∧ S2 pairs ≥ 6 ) )
```

## 4. Breadth `B`

Breadth rates systemic barrier reach across function-group pairs, derived from two counts and
capped at 2.

- **Blocked pairs.** Function-group pairs stopped by an S3 barrier or by stacked S2 barriers.
- **S2 count.** The number of S2 findings.

```
blocked = | { (fn, g) : s_d = S3 } ∪ { (fn, g) : stacked(fn, g) ≥ 3 } |
harder  = | { d : s_d = S2 } |

B       = min( 2,  step(blocked) + [ harder ≥ 3 ] )
step(n) = 2 if n ≥ 4,  1 if n ≥ 2,  0 otherwise
B      := 2   if S = S?                            the fallback with no evidence at all
```

Under the fallback, `B` is as found from the findings on file, or 2 with no evidence at all,
since the barriers are then assumed to reach widely.

## 5. Veracity `V`

Veracity rates evidence reliability. Most intake findings rest on the ACR, a vendor claim
ranked E1- on any failed quality check.

An ACR passes eight quality checks to rank E1. Any failed check drops it to E1-.

- Reports WCAG 2.0 only (the Section 508 edition) for a web or mobile product.
- No month and year, no product version, or a different version from the one being bought.
- Older than the ACR maximum age in section 10, or older than the last major release.
- No evaluation methods named.
- "Partially Supports" or "Does Not Support" without remarks.
- "Supports" throughout, or on complex criteria without remarks.
- "Not Evaluated" outside the AAA table.
- Does not cover every interface or platform being bought.

A report marked Supports on every criterion fails the sixth check. A perfect result is as
likely to mean the evaluation was not done as that no barrier exists, so the report earns
E1 only when its remarks show the work.

| `E` | Evidence | `V` |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Nothing, or marketing statements | 0 |
| E1- | Vendor ACR that fails a quality check | 1 |
| E1 | Vendor ACR that passes every quality check | 2 |
| E1+ | E1, plus the GSA issue-level supplement | 3 |
| E2a | Live vendor demonstration of essential functions with assistive technology | 4 |
| E2b | Third-party audit with a named method | 4 |
| E3 | ATI team testing of essential functions with a named method | 5 |

`Vmin`, the Veracity floor, rises with Exposure and never falls below 4 at S1, since minimal
impact needs demonstration rather than assumption.

```
Vmin(X)     = 0 if X = low,  2 if X = medium,  4 if X = high
Vmin(X, S1) = max( 4, Vmin(X) )
Vmin(X, S)  = Vmin(X)        for every other rated S
Vmin(X, S?) = ∞              an unknown Severity is never confirmed
```

Veracity gates every review before the risk grade. Severity read from the evidence on file is a
minimum until `V ≥ Vmin(X, S)`.

- **At or above the floor.** Severity is confirmed and the risk grade is read.
- **Below the floor.** Hold 3 applies. The ATI review team raises the evidence with the
  requesting unit and the vendor, re-rates Severity and resumes at the Severity step. A
  demonstration or ATI testing ranks 4 or 5, which clears every floor, so one loop closes it.
- **Below the floor, and the evidence cannot be raised (`U`).** The review falls back:
  `S := S3 (provisional)`, the barriers found are a minimum, `B` is as found, or 2 with no
  evidence at all, and the TAAP standard is **provisional**. Unproven conformance is treated as
  hiding a blocking barrier, so the TAAP is the remedy. Deny stands on the evidence in hand,
  because evidence below the floor can hide barriers but not invent them.

## 6. Holds

A hold pauses the review without a final decision until a named party supplies what the review
waits for. Holds 1 to 3, priority-ordered and mutually exclusive, gate the risk grade, and hold 3
resumes at the Severity step. Holds 4 and 5 arise in the remedy step, hold 4 for a TAAP and hold 5
for a written determination on an exception claim.

```
1.  S = S?  ∧  ¬U                              → On hold for accessibility evidence
2.  band(known answers) ≠ band(all answers)
      ∧  max floor minimum < band(all answers)  → On hold for exposure answers
3.  V < Vmin(X, S)  ∧  ¬U                      → On hold for stronger evidence
    ( S = S?  ∨  V < Vmin(X, S) )  ∧  U        → S := S3 (provisional), continue
4.  REMEDY gives On hold (no TAAP on file)      → On hold for a TAAP
5.  REMEDY gives On hold (exception claimed)    → On hold for a written determination
```

`band(known answers)` drops the unknown answers, and a group with no known answer ranks low.
An unknown answer only raises the band, so the inequality in hold 2 means that the unknown
answers alone raise `X` above every floor.

Each hold carries a heading for the requester, a holder, the party whose action releases
the hold, and the step at which the review resumes.

| Hold | Heading | Held by | Resumes at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | On hold for accessibility evidence | The requesting unit | The evidence check |
| 2 | On hold for exposure answers | The requesting unit | The Exposure check |
| 3 | On hold for stronger evidence | The ATI review team | The Severity step, with the new findings or the fallback |
| 4 | On hold for a TAAP | The requesting unit | The remedy step |
| 5 | On hold for a written determination | The president or a designee with budget authority | The remedy step |

## 7. Risk grade

The grid maps each Exposure and Severity pair to one risk grade.

```
risk_grade = GRID[ X ][ S ]
```

| `X` | S0 | S1 | S2 | S3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | No alternate access required | No alternate access required | No alternate access required | Alternate access required |
| medium | No alternate access required | No alternate access required | Alternate access required | Alternate access required |
| high | No alternate access required | No alternate access required | Alternate access required | Alternate access required |

## 8. Remedy

The remedy step maps a No alternate access required risk grade to an Approve final decision, and a TAAP
required risk grade to Approve with TAAP, Deny, hold 4 or hold 5 by the TAAP on file and the exception
status against the TAAP standard.

The TAAP standard `k`, the minimum count of TAAP requirements a plan must meet, derives from
**pressure**, the sum of the Exposure rank and Breadth. A full TAAP meets all six.

```
P    = rank(X) + B                   0 to 4
k(P) = 3  if P ≤ 1
       4  if P = 2
       6  if P ≥ 3
```

The **shortfall** is `max(0, k − met)`, with `met` the count of requirements the TAAP on file
meets.

The remedy rows apply in order, and the first matching row sets the final decision.

```
REMEDY( No alternate access required ) = Approve
REMEDY( Alternate access required )    =
    Deny                if S = S3 ∧ M                       more accessible product
    On hold (hold 4)    if no TAAP on file                  no TAAP on file
    Approve with TAAP   if met ≥ k                          standard met
    Deny                if met = 0                          no alternative access, at any S
    Approve with TAAP   if T.exception = determined         Non-Equal Alternative under 35.204
    On hold (hold 5)    if T.exception = claimed            referred for a formal determination
    Deny                if met < k                          below the standard, at any S and P
```

A TAAP below six requirements is a Non-Equal Alternative, with named support required for each
missed requirement. Below the standard, a Non-Equal Alternative is approved only under the
exception, which adds the written determination to the requirements of the approval. The
standard scales with pressure so that purchases with less at stake get a lower standard, and
the shortfall against it is stated in the denial and in the hold, so the signers report what
the plan does instead of ticking requirements it does not meet.

The TAAP on file falls in one of three states against the minimum, each with a fixed outcome.

| TAAP on file | Outcome | Warning |
|---|---|---|
| Below the minimum | Deny, unless a written undue burden or fundamental alteration determination is on file. A plan that meets none of the six is denied even then, since it gives no alternative access | None, or from pressure plus the gap under the exception |
| At the minimum, short of all six | Approve with TAAP. Where the minimum is all six, at pressure 3 or more, a plan short of six is below the minimum and is denied | From pressure plus the gap to a full TAAP |
| Full, all six | Approve with TAAP | From pressure alone, elevated from pressure 2 and high at pressure 4, so every high Exposure carries one |

The **warning level** derives from the sum of pressure and the **gap**, the count of
requirements the plan misses short of a full TAAP, with a gap of zero under no TAAP. TAAP
coverage manages barriers without removing them, so no TAAP lowers the warning below the
pressure level.

```
gap     = 6 − met
W       = P + gap
warning = low       if W < 2
          elevated  if 2 ≤ W < 4
          high      if W ≥ 4
```

Under the constants in section 10, two consequences follow.

- Below the minimum, `met ≤ k − 1`, so `gap ≥ 7 − k(P)` and `W ≥ 4` at every pressure. A plan
  approved under the exception always carries a high warning.
- A full TAAP has `gap = 0`, so `W = P`: elevated from pressure 2 and high at pressure 4.

Each level has a fixed meaning for the purchaser and a required action. The pressure and the
gap print with the word, so the label never stands alone.

| Level | `W` | What it tells the purchaser | What the purchaser does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | `W < 2` | A small or optional audience, and a plan that covers equally effective access. The residual risk is the ordinary risk of a temporary plan. | The TAAP is reviewed annually. |
| Elevated | `2 ≤ W < 4` | Either the audience is large or required, or the plan leaves named requirements uncovered. The department carries support for those requirements until the vendor's roadmap closes the barriers. | The Dean or Division Vice President signs knowing what the plan leaves to staff support and to the vendor's roadmap. The TAAP is reviewed at the first roadmap milestone or at 12 months, whichever comes first. |
| High | `W ≥ 4` | A large or required audience depends on staff support for access the plan does not provide, or on a full plan at the highest exposure. The Title II duty continues and rests on the department's capacity and the vendor's remediation. | Weigh the more accessible product before buying. The Dean or Division Vice President acknowledges the residual risk in the TAAP. The TAAP is reviewed at the first roadmap milestone. |

The warning sentence in the review states, in order: the level with its numbers, who depends on
the plan and for which essential functions (cannot perform at S3, only with clearly more time or
effort at S2), the audience the pressure counts, what the department carries until the vendor's
roadmap closes the barriers, and the action. The gap term follows the TAAP template's
Institutional Risk tiers, which grade a plan by the requirements it meets. The pressure term and
the cuts are the matrix's, with the Section508.gov risk model as the nearest precedent. The
template's tiers grade the plan alone and are a different measure, so the matrix never prints
that phrase for the warning level. The sources are collected in `warning-level-research.md`.

## 9. Requirements by final decision

`R` marks a CSU Vendor Accessibility Roadmap requirement.

```
R = hold 4  ∨  hold 5  ∨  final_decision = Approve with TAAP
    ∨ ( final_decision = Approve  ∧  S ≠ S0  ∧  X ∈ {medium, high} )
```

Only S1 reaches the last line, since every No alternate access required risk grade at medium or high Exposure is S0
or S1.

- **Accommodation scope.** An approval under `A` states that it is limited to the
  accommodation, and use of the product beyond the named person returns it to review.
- **Standard contract terms.** Every approval carries four terms.
  - CSU General Provisions for IT Acquisitions, clause 45
  - Complaint resolution under Gov Code 7405(b)
  - Regular ACR updates
  - A vendor-published accessibility statement for the product
- **Roadmap terms.** Under `R`, the approval also carries two terms.
  - Roadmap milestones in the contract
  - A vendor demonstration of progress against the milestones
- **Day-one service.** At high Exposure and S3, the plan serves every affected person from the
  first day of use.
- **Best-meets record.** At high Exposure and S3 for a non-web or mixed product, a best-meets
  record with market research (Section 508 E202.7).
- **TAAP accessibility statement.** A campus-written statement in every TAAP, separate from
  the vendor's statement and posted wherever the product is in use. The statement covers
  known barriers, impacted disability groups, a reference to the TAAP, a disclaimer about
  unknown barriers and a support contact.
- **Exception record.** Under a Non-Equal Alternative, the written undue
  burden or fundamental alteration determination by the president or a designee (28 CFR 35.164
  and 35.204; Section 508 E202.6), listing the support that remains for each missed requirement.
- **Validation at review.** Under the fallback, the first TAAP review re-rates Severity on
  evidence at the Veracity floor, which the vendor demonstration under the roadmap terms
  supplies.
- **TAAP signers.** Three signatures on every TAAP.
  - Department Chair or Manager
  - Dean or Division Vice President
  - ADA Compliance Officer

| Final decision or hold | Requires | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Approve | The standard contract terms, plus the roadmap terms under `R` | Until renewal, with a new review at renewal |
| Approve with TAAP | The standard contract terms, the roadmap terms, the TAAP accessibility statement, and signatures from all three TAAP signers, plus day-one service and the best-meets record at high Exposure and S3, the exception record under the exception, and validation at review under the fallback | 12 months or until renewal, whichever comes first, with annual TAAP review across a multi-year contract |
| Deny | The denial reasons, the applicable TAAP standard, and the path to a new review | None |
| Hold 4 | Submission of a TAAP at or above the TAAP standard, followed by the Approve with TAAP terms | Open until a TAAP is on file |
| Hold 5 | The exception record, followed by the Approve with TAAP terms under a high warning | Open until the determination is on file |
| Holds 1 to 3 | Submission of what the hold waits for, or the ATI review team's record that the evidence cannot be raised | Open until the documentation or the record is on file |
