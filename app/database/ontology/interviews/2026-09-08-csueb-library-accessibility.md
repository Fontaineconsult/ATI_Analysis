## The walkthrough spine

SF State's library interview on 2026-08-07 produced a usable anatomy for this indicator.
Ask CSUEB the same questions. Do not assume the answers match, because the point of
asking is to find where they diverge.

1. **Acquisition pathways.** SF State turned out to have three: local subscription and
   purchase, open-access additions at a subject liaison's discretion, and Chancellor's
   Office central purchasing, which splits into the Electronic Core Collection and SDLC
   resources campuses opt into. Roughly two thirds of their acquisitions came through the
   Chancellor's Office. Ask what CSUEB's pathways are and roughly how the volume splits.
   The split matters, because centrally purchased products are procured by the
   Chancellor's Office rather than by the campus, which changes who answers for them.
2. **What happens to the conformance report.** The CSUEB note says Procurement requires
   VPAT reviews before purchase. SF State's library collects conformance reports because a
   purchase cannot proceed without one, and performs no substantive review of them, which
   is a different fact from "reviews happen." Ask directly: when a report arrives, who
   reads it, and what would cause a purchase to stop?
3. **Digitization and conversion.** Who requests, who does the work, what tool, what
   record. This is where the Learning Commons and Accessible Media services meet, and
   where existing CSUEB machinery may already be carrying the library.
4. **When a patron reports a barrier.** SF State routes these through library chat and
   email to an electronic resources team, who contact the vendor, with no stated service
   level and a low absolute volume. Ask where a CSUEB report lands, who owns the vendor
   conversation, and whether anything is counted.
5. **Maintenance.** The indicator says maintaining, not only acquiring. Ask what happens
   at renewal, and whether a product that degraded is ever caught.
