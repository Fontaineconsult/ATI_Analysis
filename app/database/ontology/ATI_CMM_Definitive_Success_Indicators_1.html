Accessible Technology Initiative

ATI Capability Maturity Model
Goals and Success Indicators

This page presents the authoritative current success indicators and explains how they relate to the indicators previously published on the ATI website.

About this update

The ATI Capability Maturity Model has been reorganized to reflect the current structure of accessibility responsibilities across the California State University. The current framework contains 65 authoritative success indicators organized under five priority areas.

Use this page for current implementation and reporting. The legacy indicators are included in the crosswalk below for transition purposes. When the legacy and current wording differ, the current indicator listed above is authoritative.

Current success indicators

Expand a goal to view its current indicators. The numbering is intentionally non-sequential because some indicators were removed, moved, consolidated, or newly added during the update.

Web

Goal 1: Web Accessibility Evaluation Process
  • 1.2Develop a process to Inventory all websites, applications (web or mobile) affiliated with the university, including those developed by contract vendors.
  • 1.4Develop a process to perform regularly scheduled accessibility evaluations to identify campus-affiliated websites and applications (web or mobile) that need remediation.
  • 1.6Develop a process to conduct manual accessibility evaluations on campus-affiliated websites and applications (web or mobile).
  • 1.7Develop a process to distribute evaluation results to campus members and/or vendors responsible for website maintenance.
  • 1.9Ensure that campus members involved in maintaining web content (including social media, videos, electronic documents) and web/mobile applications understand the accessibility evaluation process.
  • 1.19Develop a process for application-specific accessibility statements for third party products and platforms in use that have known accessibility barriers
  • 1.20Establish a process to identify all campus-affiliated social media accounts and assess the accessibility of their shared content in alignment with recognized standards.
  • 1.21Develop a process to implement and regularly review accessibility statements in alignment with CSU Chancellor’s Office guidance, ensuring statements are clearly posted on all campus-affiliated websites, applications, and digital platforms.
Goal 2: New Website/Web Application and Digital Content Design and Development Process
  • 2.1Develop a process to ensure that accessibility is integrated at all stages of the website/application and mobile development lifecycle, including requirements-gathering, design, development, testing, and deployment, to meet Section 508 and WCAG standards
  • 2.10Establish a process for content editors to request an accessibility review of new web content, electronic documents, audio/video materials, and social media posts prior to publication, ensuring compliance with WCAG 2.1 AA standards.
Goal 3: Ongoing Monitoring Process
  • 3.2Develop a process to verify that any changes made to existing websites, web applications, mobile apps, electronic documents, and audio and video comply with Section 508 accessibility standards.
  • 3.7Develop a process that combines automated web accessibility scanning tools with manual testing methods to identify accessibility barriers.
Goal 4: Evaluate Digital Content & Electronic Documents Held on the Web
  • 1.11Develop a process to conduct manual accessibility evaluations on electronic documents (word processor produced, spreadsheets, presentations, PDFs).
  • 1.12Develop a process to conduct manual evaluations on multimedia digital content (video and audio).
  • 1.14Develop a process to distribute evaluation results to campus members and vendors responsible for electronic document maintenance.
  • 1.15Ensure that campus members involved in maintaining electronic documents are familiar with the accessibility evaluation process and accessibility requirements.

Instructional Materials

Goal 1: Timely Adoption of Instructional Materials
  • 1.2Develop a process (e.g., developed and documented practices, specified staff time, educational/training resources, and technology) to achieve compliance with timely adoption.
  • 1.6Develop a process to distribute timely adoption performance reports to campus administration each semester.
Goal 4: Instructor Use of LMS (or non-LMS) Course Websites
  • 4.1Develop a process to promote the posting of accessible instructional materials to the university approved LMS.
  • 4.3Develop a process and document specific guidelines and procedures for creating accessible course content hosted in the campus LMS.
  • 4.5Develop a process for accommodation staff to receive timely access, such as appropriate LMS roles and permissions, to instructional materials within the university-approved learning management system (LMS).
  • 4.11Develop a process to review the accessibility of faculty-maintained websites or web applications, whether hosted on the campus domain or elsewhere.
Goal 5: Accessibility Requirements for Multimedia, Interactive Content, and Emerging Instructional Technologies
  • 5.11Develop a process for creating, selecting, adopting, and remediating audio and video assets.
  • 5.14Develop a process for providing equal access to learning outcomes from emerging instructional technologies.
  • 5.15Develop a process for creating, selecting, adopting, and remediating publisher created content.
  • 5.16Develop a process for creating, selecting, adopting, and remediating learning and engagement tools.
  • 5.17Develop a process to provide accessible course syllabi at the beginning of each term and promote use of the university approved LMS.
Goal 6: Accessibility Requirements for Course Review and Remediation
  • 6.2Develop a process for selecting, authoring, and procuring accessible course materials (new course).
  • 6.4Develop a process to facilitate an accessibility review and remediate course material before posting (course revision).
  • 6.5Develop a process for faculty and instructional staff to incorporate accessibility at the time of course redesign (complete course redesign).
  • 6.7Develop a process to conduct regularly scheduled accessibility evaluations using automated tools and manual techniques to identify course content that requires remediation.
  • 6.8Develop a process to prioritize and remediate inaccessible course content.
  • 6.9Develop Temporary Alternate Access Plans (TAAPs) that address how the campus will ensure equal access for individuals with digital course content with known accessibility barriers.
  • 6.10The campus has established a process to evaluate the accessibility of Learning Tools Interoperability (LTI) integrations and external applications used within the campus Learning Management System (LMS), including publisher courseware and digital learning platforms.
Goal 7: Creation and Adoption of Accessible Instructional Materials
  • 7.11Establish collaborative processes between digital accessibility teams and library services to ensure that accessibility is proactively addressed when acquiring, converting, digitizing, creating, and maintaining digital library assets used in course content.
  • 7.12Develop a process with faculty development centers for training and resources that support faculty and instructional staff in designing accessible instructional materials and content.

Procurement

Goal 1: Procurement Processes
  • 1.3Develop and implement a process to assess risk based on the impact and likelihood that new ICT procurements and renewals could present a barrier.
  • 1.4Develop a process to determine the level/complexity of Section 508 evaluations that will be required for new procurements and/or renewals.
  • 1.8Develop a process for validating accessibility conformance claims (ACRs, Roadmaps, Accessibility Statements, accessibility demonstrations, RFP responses, and testing documentation).
  • 1.9Develop a process for working collaboratively with vendors during the procurement process to assess and improve the accessibility of their product (i.e., ACR review process, Accessibility Roadmap).
  • 1.12Develop a risk-informed process to evaluate Section 508 compliance for acquisitions, regardless of procurement method or cost, (e.g. competitive and non-competitive bids, purchase card transactions, auxiliary, technology that students are required to purchase, LTIs, MEAs,
Goal 4: Temporary Alternate Access Plans (TAAP)
  • 4.6Campuses have established procedures to ensure the consistent implementation of the current standardized ATI Temporary Alternate Access Plan (TAAP) template (formerly EEAAP) whenever ICT products or services in use present known accessibility barriers.
Goal 8: Experience/Implementation
  • 8.11The campus, including auxiliaries and affiliated entities, systematically identifies and addresses gaps across all procurement channels to ensure all acquired ICT undergoes the accessible procurement process.
  • 8.12Develop a process for ICT purchases routed through the CSU Systemwide procurement platform to undergo the same accessibility conformance review as those reviewed through campus ICT procurement procedures.

Communication & Training (NEW)

Goal 1: Promote a Campus-Wide Culture of Accessibility
  • 1.1Develop a structured, ongoing campus-wide communication campaign (with executive support) to raise awareness about digital accessibility responsibilities and resources.
  • 1.2Incorporate digital accessibility awareness into onboarding and orientation programs for new faculty, staff, students, and student employees.
  • 1.3Develop a communication process to ensure all digital content creators (e.g., those who create web, instructional materials, marketing, social media, electronic documents, video, audio) know where to get training, help, and resources for accessibility compliance.
  • 1.4Develop a process so that vendors and campus members involved in ICT procurements know who to contact for assistance, resources, and support.
Goal 2: Ongoing Role-Specific Accessibility Training
  • 2.1Develop a comprehensive training process for web and mobile developers, designers, content contributors, and digital communicators, e.g., document creators, social media managers, and marketing staff, that covers Section 508 standards and campus-specific monitoring processes.
  • 2.2Develop a training process (with varied content and modalities) for faculty and instructional staff on the creation, adoption, and remediation of accessible instructional materials.
  • 2.3Integrate accessibility into ongoing academic technology training through faculty support centers.
  • 2.4Develop training to ensure everyone purchasing IT products and services understands relevant policies, procedures, and accessibility requirements (This can include purchase requestors, administrative support staff, technology support staff, buyers, procurement officers, and purchase cardholders).
  • 2.5Develop a process to ensure that employees with explicit ATI accessibility responsibilities in each priority area (Web, Instructional Materials, and Procurement) are prioritized for ongoing, role-specific professional development to meet evolving accessibility standards and campus goals.

Governance, Planning, and Policies (NEW)

Goal 1: Establish Clear Roles and Responsibilities for Accessibility Oversight
  • 1.1Assigned authority and responsibility for accessibility documented in appropriate position descriptions
  • 1.2Assigned authority and responsibility for the web evaluation process and ongoing monitoring (to include digital content ) to a body (person(s) or business entity).
  • 1.3Assigned authority and responsibility for the new web/mobile development process (to include updates and maintenance) to a body (person(s) or business entity).
  • 1.4Assigned authority and responsibility for the accessible ICT procurement process to a body (person(s) or business entity).
  • 1.5Assigned authority and responsibility for tracking, reporting and distributing timely adoption metrics for instructional material
  • 1.6Assigned authority and responsibility for ongoing monitoring of accessibility reports on Instructional material within the LMS to a body (person(s) or business entity).
  • 1.7Assigned authority and responsibility for accessibility training program(s) to a body (person(s) or business entities).
Goal 2: Formalize Accessibility-Related Policies and Procedures
  • 2.1Campus has formally documented (e.g., Policy, Resolution, or Procedure) a process to ensure the timely adoption of textbooks and other instructional materials.
  • 2.2Campus has formally documented (through Policy or Resolution) faculty responsibility for selecting and authoring accessible instructional materials.
  • 2.3The campus has formally documented (through Policy, Resolution, or Procedure) responsibility for accessible web/mobile and digital communications
  • 2.4The Campus has formally documented (through Policy, Resolution or Procedure) responsibility for procuring accessible ICT
Goal 3: Embed Accessibility into Institutional Planning Cycles
  • 3.1Develop a process for the ATI Steering Committee and subcommittees to review, revise and approve the updated Annual Campus Accessible Technology Plan, IT Strategic plan, campus strategic plan.

Crosswalk status definitions

Retained / revised
The underlying responsibility remains in the current framework, with updated wording, scope, or terminology.
Moved
The responsibility remains, but is now located under another current priority area.
Consolidated
Multiple legacy indicators are addressed through one or more broader current indicators.
Removed
The legacy indicator is no longer part of the authoritative current set.
